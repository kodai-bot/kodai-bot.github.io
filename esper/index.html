Esper is an explorer for archaeological evidence, modelled on the photo-analysis machine in Blade Runner. Every record (a monument, a place-name in an early Irish text, a radiocarbon date) is treated as a signal: something observed at a place, with a span of time behind it. You move through them by territory and by depth, and see how each one connects to its neighbours in space, time and name.

This page runs the real Esper interface over a frozen snapshot of Co. Sligo: 6,612 signals across its six baronies. Nothing is sent anywhere, and nothing can be changed. Best on a laptop or desktop screen.

  1. Click a barony in Sligo (they glow amber on hover), then press load.
  2. Click a signal: its worldline, record and φ spectrograph appear.
  3. Press Draw relations to see what it is linked to nearby, in time, and by name. Try Drumcliff, north of Sligo town: round tower, high cross and church.
  4. The 1km · 100m · 10m · 1m buttons change the grid as you zoom in. Map shows a basemap underneath.
