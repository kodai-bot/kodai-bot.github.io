Cuéllar's nights

On 21 September 1588 three ships of the Spanish Armada were driven ashore at Streedagh, Co. Sligo, and broke up within an hour. More than a thousand men drowned. Captain Francisco de Cuéllar got ashore. A year later, safe in Antwerp, he wrote down what happened.

His letter is full of small, unguarded details: a clock time at night, daybreak, a number of days, "we awaited the rising of the moon." Here each one is coded as a signal, all of them are solved together into a dated path through time, and every mention of light is tested against the sun and moon as they really were.

Two lights, two verdicts

✓ passes · S040

"about one o'clock in the night, more than two hundred horsemen"

They rode down to the wrecks and passed him without seeing him. That night the new moon, 2% lit, had set at 18:25. At one in the morning there was no moon at all.

The darkness that hid him is computed, not guessed.

✗ fails · S083

"we awaited the rising of the moon"

As the letter tells it, this night in the oat hut falls between 25 and 30 September. On every one of those evenings a waxing moon is already in the sky at dusk. There was no moonrise to wait for.

The first evening with a moonrise after dark is 5 October. So either a week or more passes unrecorded before the oat huts, or the detail is misremembered. The signal was coded low-trust; now the low trust has a cause.
